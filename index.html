About / 00

I’m interested in the moment an abstract model becomes something you can point to: a surface state, a bent track, a clean curve, or a piece of hardware on the bench.

My current research connects crystal symmetry to observable electronic structure. I classify interfaces by their symmetry operations, track the chiral charges of multifold band nodes, and turn expensive quantum simulations into cheaper, physically transparent models.

Long term, I want to make high-throughput, machine-learning-driven materials discovery practical—using models that learn from first-principles data without giving up the physics that makes their predictions meaningful.

Selected work / 01—03

Current research projects.

01

Computational condensed matter

Learning the landscape of chiral grain boundaries

In the Quantum Chiraltronics Group at USF, I study how grain-boundary geometry changes Weyl points, electron transport, and surface states in topological chiral intermetallics. A high-throughput Python and ASE pipeline maps more than 13,000 B20 CoGe boundaries, using symmetry-aware descriptors to choose the most informative structures for first-principles calculations.

Boundary → symmetry → state
02

First-principles electronic structure

Domain walls in magnetic topological insulators

X-MTI is a first-principles test of controllable domain walls in magnetic topological insulators. I use all-electron density functional theory to study a simplified Co-doped Bi2Se3 interface with nickel or iron and ask which magnetic arrangements can support useful, localized electronic behavior.

Structure → magnetism → transport
03

Experimental particle physics

A cosmic-ray telescope, built as a digital twin first

I am developing a two-counter scintillator muon telescope with a permanent-magnet spectrometer for charge discrimination. COMSOL field maps feed a Geant4 Monte Carlo so the geometry, trigger acceptance, and μ+/μ− separation can be tuned before the first physical part is assembled.

Simulate → measure → build
