About Projects

Calvin

Physics & Math Undergraduate at the University of South Florida

B.S. Physics and B.A. Mathematics (pure concentration) at the University of South Florida, expected 2028. I work in computational condensed matter physics and experimental particle physics.

I do research in the Quantum Chiraltronics Group at USF, studying how grain boundary geometry affects Weyl points and the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction in the B20 chiral magnet CoGe. I use density functional theory and Wannier-based tight-binding models to predict which boundaries and twin interfaces host topologically protected electronic states.

My scientific interests center on connecting crystal symmetry to observable electronic structure: classifying interfaces by their symmetry operations, tracking the chiral charges of multifold band nodes, and turning expensive quantum simulations into cheap, physically transparent models.

Alongside theory work, I build and simulate hardware. I am developing a two-counter scintillator cosmic-ray muon telescope with a permanent-magnet spectrometer for charge discrimination, designed end-to-end in a Geant4 Monte Carlo simulation before construction.

Looking ahead, I aspire to develop the computational methods that make high-throughput, machine-learning-driven materials discovery practical. Interface and boundary calculations are among the most difficult and handcrafted problems in computational condensed matter; my goal is to build symmetry-aware machine learning models that learn electronic structure directly from first-principles data — turning months of calculation into seconds of inference without giving up the physics.

Selected Projects

ML-Driven Discovery in Chiral Magnets

Quantum Chiraltronics Group at USF. A high-throughput Python/ASE pipeline evaluating 13,000+ B20 CoGe grain boundaries: SOAP descriptors and farthest-point sampling map the structural latent space, and targeted VASP calculations on the CIRCE cluster build the DFT baseline for machine-learned topological tight-binding models.

Screening for Endotaxial Materials

NSF REU, Wenhao Sun Lab, University of Michigan. High-throughput ab initio screening of 150+ undocumented 2D TMD phases for thermo-driven phase transitions and charge density waves, validating an inexpensive Debye-plus-elastic-tensor phonon model to screen transitions across thousands of candidate materials.

X-MTI: Domain Walls in Magnetic Topological Insulators

A first-principles validation of a proposal for controllable domain walls in magnetic topological insulators, using all-electron DFT (FLEUR) on a simplified model of Co-doped Bi₂Se₃ interfaced with nickel or iron.

Cosmic-Ray Muon Telescope with Charge Discrimination

Society of Physics Students at USF — Data Analysis Lead. A two-counter coincidence telescope — a slit trigger counter above an N52 permanent-magnet spectrometer and a CosmicWatch-style detector below — that separates μ⁺ from μ− by their opposite deflection in the magnet gap. The instrument was designed as its own digital twin first: a COMSOL finite-element map of the magnet field is imported into a Geant4 Monte Carlo with cosmic-ray showers, which tunes the geometry, quantifies μ⁺/μ− separation, and sets the discriminator edge before any hardware is built.
